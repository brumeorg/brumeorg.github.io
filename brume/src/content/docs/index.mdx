      <p class="brume-feature-detail">Apply column-level pseudonymization with the strategy that fits each field. FAKE for realistic synthetic values, MASK for partial reveals, HASH for one-way hashing, NULLIFY
           to drop sensitive data, FPE_ID / FPE_UUID for format-preserving encryption of identifiers, or DATE_SHIFT to move dates while keeping their distribution. Every transformation is deterministic and keyed by your hmac-secret and fpe-key:
            same input + same secret = same output, every time, across all releases of a major version. Two runs produce identical results — your tests stay stable, your debug sessions stay coherent across rebuilds.</p>

      <h3>Keep your constraint</h3>
      <p class="brume-feature-summary">Foreign keys stay valid end-to-end. No disabled triggers, no broken joins.</p>
      <p class="brume-feature-detail">Foreign keys stay valid on the target database. No disabled triggers, no suspended integrity checks, no patching broken joins after the fact. Format-preserving encryption
           rewrites primary keys and the FKs that point to them consistently, so referential integrity holds end-to-end. And when your business logic spans tables that aren't formally
           joined (e.g. an email shared between users and audit_logs), linked_columns guarantees the same real value produces the same fake value across all of them.</p>
    </div>

      <p class="brume-feature-summary">Row-level filters with automatic FK traversal — a self-contained subset of your data.</p>
      <p class="brume-feature-detail">Define row-level filters on any table (created_at >= '2025-01-01', tenant_id = 42…) and let Brume copy every row within fk_depth foreign-key hops of them, towards parents and
